Title: Private Credit Market Dynamics - Opportunity and Risk
Date: 2025-03-05
Category: commentary
Source: Oaktree Capital Memo

Private credit has grown from $500 billion to $1.7 trillion in assets under management over the past decade, filling the void left by bank retrenchment. This growth creates opportunity for investors but also raises questions about underwriting standards, liquidity, and systemic risk.

STRUCTURAL DRIVERS

Post-GFC bank regulations constrained traditional lending, particularly for leveraged buyouts and middle-market companies. Private credit funds with permanent capital stepped in, offering speed and certainty of execution. Sponsors prefer private lenders despite higher rates for flexibility and relationship value.

RETURN EXPECTATIONS

Current direct lending yields of 11-12% on senior secured loans compare favorably to broadly syndicated loans at 8-9% and high yield bonds at 7-8%. The illiquidity premium has expanded as traditional fixed income rallied. Net returns to LPs of 8-10% are achievable with current yields and modest losses.

RISK FACTORS

Several risks warrant attention. First, underwriting standards may have loosened as capital flooded into the asset class. Second, documentation has weakened with covenant-lite structures becoming standard. Third, valuations are mark-to-model with potential gaps to realizable values. Fourth, liquidity mismatches exist in some vehicles.

CREDIT CYCLE POSITIONING

We are later cycle with defaults rising from lows. Default rates should reach 3-4% for private credit, below historical averages given borrower quality. Loss severities may be higher than expected due to aggressive leverage and weak covenants. Manager selection becomes critical.

OPPORTUNITY SET

The best opportunities are in complexity premium - situations requiring operational expertise, sector specialization, or creative structuring. Avoid commodity senior lending where competition has compressed returns. We are selectively adding to stressed and distressed strategies ahead of maturity walls.
