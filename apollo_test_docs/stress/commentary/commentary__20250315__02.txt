Title: Japan's Economic Renaissance - A Structural Shift
Date: 2025-03-15
Category: commentary
Source: GMO Quarterly Letter

After three decades of stagnation, Japan appears to be experiencing a genuine structural transformation. The combination of inflation returning, corporate governance reforms, and currency weakness creates an opportunity set we have not seen since the 1980s. We are significantly overweight Japanese equities.

END OF DEFLATION

Japan has achieved what seemed impossible - sustainable inflation above 1% with rising nominal wages. Core CPI has exceeded 2% for over a year while base wages grew 3.5% in 2024, the highest in three decades. This transforms the valuation framework for Japanese assets, which had been anchored by deflation expectations.

CORPORATE GOVERNANCE REVOLUTION

Tokyo Stock Exchange reforms are forcing companies to address capital efficiency. Firms trading below book value must disclose plans to improve ROE. Share buybacks reached record levels in 2024. Cross-shareholdings continue unwinding. Foreign investors are rewarding reformed companies with multiple expansion.

CURRENCY TAILWIND

The weak yen enhances competitiveness for exporters and drives foreign tourist spending. Japanese manufacturers are investing in domestic capacity after years of offshoring. The terms of trade shock from yen weakness creates distributional challenges but overall economic tailwind.

STRUCTURAL VERSUS CYCLICAL

Skeptics argue this is another false dawn for Japan. We disagree. The combination of policy changes, demographic adjustments (more women and seniors working), and inflation expectations makes this cycle different. Japan is transitioning from a deflationary asset to a reflationary opportunity.

VALUATIONS

Japanese equities trade at 14x forward earnings versus 21x for US and 13x for Europe. Despite recent gains, valuations remain attractive relative to improved fundamentals. We see 30-40% upside over three years from earnings growth and multiple expansion.
