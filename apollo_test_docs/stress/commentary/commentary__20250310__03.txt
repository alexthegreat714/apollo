Title: The Changing Nature of Recession Risk
Date: 2025-03-10
Category: commentary
Source: Apollo Chief Economist Weekly

The traditional recession indicators that have reliably predicted downturns for decades are flashing warnings, yet the economy continues expanding. This apparent contradiction reflects structural changes that require updating our recession models and risk assessment frameworks.

INVERTED YIELD CURVE

The yield curve inverted in July 2022 and has remained so for over 30 months. Historically, recession follows inversion by 12-18 months. Yet GDP growth has remained positive and employment robust. The curve may be distorted by QT, term premium changes, and expectations of Fed pivot rather than signaling recession.

LABOR MARKET SIGNALS

Leading indicators like temporary help employment and initial claims have weakened, typically precursors to broader employment deterioration. However, job openings remain elevated and unemployment is low. The labor market appears to be normalizing from pandemic extremes rather than contracting.

CORPORATE PROFIT MARGINS

S&P 500 profit margins remain near record highs despite predictions of compression from wage growth and input costs. Companies have successfully passed through prices and realized productivity gains. Margin resilience removes a typical recessionary driver.

STRUCTURAL CHANGES

Several structural factors may be extending the cycle: excess household savings buffering consumption, strong corporate balance sheets limiting defaults, demographic shifts affecting labor supply, and housing lock-in effects from rate dynamics. The cycle may be longer but not eliminated.

INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

Do not position for imminent recession based solely on traditional indicators. Maintain equity exposure but with quality and defensive tilt. Extend duration given eventual rate cuts but remain tactical around timing. Prepare for economic slowing without catastrophizing about hard landing.
