Title: Fiscal Sustainability and Market Implications
Date: 2025-03-01
Category: commentary
Source: PIMCO Secular Outlook

The trajectory of US fiscal policy is unsustainable, with debt-to-GDP exceeding 120% and deficits projected at 6-7% of GDP indefinitely. While markets have been remarkably sanguine about fiscal risks, the growing interest expense burden and limited fiscal flexibility could have significant implications for asset prices.

DEBT DYNAMICS

Federal debt held by the public reached $27 trillion in 2024 and will exceed $35 trillion by 2030 under current policy. Interest expense is the fastest-growing budget item at $900 billion annually, soon exceeding defense spending. Each 100bp increase in rates adds $250 billion to annual interest costs.

POLITICAL CONSTRAINTS

Neither party has demonstrated willingness to address the structural deficit through spending cuts or revenue increases. Mandatory spending (Social Security, Medicare, interest) consumes 75% of the budget and grows automatically. Discretionary spending is already at historical lows as a share of GDP.

MARKET PRICING

Despite record supply, Treasury yields remain moderate by historical standards. Term premium is near zero, suggesting limited compensation for duration risk. Foreign buyers have reduced purchases, with domestic demand and Fed holdings filling the gap. The marginal buyer is increasingly price-sensitive.

INFLATION AS PRESSURE VALVE

In the absence of fiscal adjustment, inflation becomes the path of least resistance for reducing the real debt burden. Financial repression - keeping rates below inflation - has historical precedent post-WWII. This argues for owning real assets and inflation protection.

INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

Underweight nominal Treasuries at current term premiums. Favor TIPS for inflation protection. International diversification becomes more valuable as US fiscal trajectory diverges from other developed markets. Shorter duration reduces exposure to fiscal premium expansion. Gold and real assets provide portfolio insurance.
