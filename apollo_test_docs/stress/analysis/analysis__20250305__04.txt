Title: Consumer Credit Deterioration Risk Assessment
Date: 2025-03-05
Category: analysis
Source: Fitch Ratings

CREDIT QUALITY OVERVIEW

US consumer credit quality continues normalizing from exceptional pandemic-era strength. Delinquency rates across credit cards, auto loans, and personal loans have risen but remain below pre-pandemic levels for prime borrowers. Subprime segments show more pronounced deterioration.

CREDIT CARD ANALYSIS

Credit card delinquencies (90+ days) reached 2.8% in Q4 2024, up from 2.0% a year ago but below the 3.2% pre-pandemic average. Charge-off rates are 4.8%, trending toward the historical average of 5.5%. Younger borrowers and those with lower incomes show steepest increases.

AUTO LOAN ASSESSMENT

Auto loan delinquencies have stabilized at 2.1% after rising through 2024. Subprime auto shows greater stress at 8.5% delinquency. Extended loan terms and negative equity positions create elevated loss severities when defaults occur.

RISK FACTORS

Key risks include labor market deterioration that increases unemployment above 4.5%, student loan payment resumption straining household budgets, and credit availability tightening that reduces refinancing options for stressed borrowers.

OUTLOOK

We maintain a stable outlook on consumer ABS and bank credit card portfolios. Normalization continues through 2025 but systemic stress is unlikely given strong labor market fundamentals. Subprime-focused lenders face the greatest pressure.

INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

Favor senior tranches of prime credit card ABS. Avoid subprime auto and personal loan exposures. Bank credit card portfolios warrant close monitoring but current reserve levels appear adequate.
