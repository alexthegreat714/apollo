Title: Reading and Analyzing a Balance Sheet
Date: 2025-03-11
Category: education
Source: Apollo Synthetic Finance Set
Priority: 4

A balance sheet presents a company's financial position at a specific point in time, displaying assets, liabilities, and shareholders' equity. The fundamental accounting equation that governs all balance sheets is:

Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity

This equation must always balance, hence the name "balance sheet."

Components breakdown:

Assets (what the company owns):
- Current assets: Cash, accounts receivable, inventory (convertible to cash within one year)
- Non-current assets: Property, plant, equipment, intangible assets

Liabilities (what the company owes):
- Current liabilities: Accounts payable, short-term debt (due within one year)
- Long-term liabilities: Bonds payable, long-term loans

Shareholders' Equity (net worth):
- Common stock, retained earnings, additional paid-in capital

Numerical Example:

XYZ Corporation Balance Sheet (in thousands):

Current Assets: $500
- Cash: $100
- Accounts Receivable: $200
- Inventory: $200

Non-Current Assets: $800
- Property & Equipment: $700
- Intangibles: $100

Total Assets: $1,300

Current Liabilities: $300
- Accounts Payable: $150
- Short-term Debt: $150

Long-term Liabilities: $400
Total Liabilities: $700

Shareholders' Equity: $600
- Common Stock: $200
- Retained Earnings: $400

Total Liabilities + Equity: $1,300

Key ratios derived from balance sheets:

Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
= $500 / $300 = 1.67

This indicates the company has $1.67 in current assets for every $1 of current liabilities.

Debt-to-Equity Ratio = Total Liabilities / Shareholders' Equity
= $700 / $600 = 1.17

This shows the company uses $1.17 of debt for every $1 of equity financing.

Balance sheets enable assessment of liquidity, solvency, and capital structure.
