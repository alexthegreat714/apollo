Title: Time Value of Money Fundamentals
Date: 2025-03-03
Category: education
Source: Apollo Synthetic Finance Set
Priority: 4

The time value of money (TVM) is a core financial principle stating that a dollar today is worth more than a dollar in the future. This concept underpins all financial valuation, from bond pricing to capital budgeting decisions.

Two primary calculations derive from TVM:

Present Value (PV): The current worth of a future sum of money.
PV = FV / (1 + r)^n

Future Value (FV): The value of a current sum at a specified future date.
FV = PV × (1 + r)^n

Where:
- r = Discount rate or interest rate per period
- n = Number of periods

Numerical Example:

You expect to receive $5,000 in 3 years. The appropriate discount rate is 8% annually. What is the present value?

PV = 5,000 / (1 + 0.08)^3
PV = 5,000 / (1.08)^3
PV = 5,000 / 1.2597
PV = $3,969.16

The present value of receiving $5,000 in three years is $3,969.16 today.

Conversely, if you invest $3,969.16 today at 8% annual interest:

FV = 3,969.16 × (1.08)^3
FV = 3,969.16 × 1.2597
FV = $5,000

Applications of TVM include:

1. Loan amortization schedules
2. Bond valuation
3. Net present value (NPV) analysis
4. Comparing investment alternatives
5. Retirement planning calculations

Annuity formulas extend TVM to series of equal payments. The present value of an ordinary annuity is:

PVA = PMT × [(1 - (1 + r)^-n) / r]

Where PMT is the periodic payment amount.

TVM calculations require consistency between the discount rate and time periods. Monthly cash flows require monthly discount rates, calculated as the annual rate divided by 12.
