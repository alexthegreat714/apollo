Title: Bond Convexity and Price Behavior
Date: 2025-03-19
Category: education
Source: Apollo Synthetic Finance Set
Priority: 4

Convexity measures the curvature of the relationship between bond prices and yields, capturing the non-linear price behavior that duration alone cannot explain. Duration provides a linear approximation, while convexity adjusts for the actual curved relationship.

The price change formula incorporating both duration and convexity:

ΔP/P ≈ -Modified Duration × Δy + (1/2) × Convexity × (Δy)²

Convexity formula:

Convexity = [Σ t(t+1) × PV(CFt)] / [Bond Price × (1+y)²]

Numerical Example:

Using the same 3-year bond (5% coupon, 4% YTM, $1,027.82 price):

Convexity calculation:
Year 1: 1(2) × $48.08 = $96.16
Year 2: 2(3) × $46.23 = $277.38
Year 3: 3(4) × $933.51 = $11,202.12

Sum = $11,575.66

Convexity = $11,575.66 / [$1,027.82 × (1.04)²]
= $11,575.66 / $1,111.94
= 10.41

For a 2% increase in yields:

Using duration only:
ΔP/P ≈ -2.75 × 0.02 = -5.50%

Using duration and convexity:
ΔP/P ≈ -2.75 × 0.02 + (1/2) × 10.41 × (0.02)²
= -0.055 + 0.5 × 10.41 × 0.0004
= -0.055 + 0.00208
= -5.29%

The convexity adjustment reduces the estimated price decline from 5.50% to 5.29%.

Properties of convexity:

1. Always positive for standard bonds (prices curve upward)
2. Larger for lower coupon bonds
3. Larger for longer maturity bonds
4. Larger for lower yield environments

Why convexity matters:

- Duration underestimates price increases when yields fall
- Duration overestimates price decreases when yields rise
- Bonds with higher convexity outperform in volatile interest rate environments
- Investors may pay a premium for convexity

Negative convexity occurs in callable bonds and mortgage-backed securities, where price appreciation is limited due to early redemption or prepayment risk.
