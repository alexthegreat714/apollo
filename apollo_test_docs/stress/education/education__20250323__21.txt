Title: Price-to-Earnings Ratio Analysis
Date: 2025-03-23
Category: education
Source: Apollo Synthetic Finance Set
Priority: 4

The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio is a widely used valuation metric that compares a company's stock price to its earnings per share. It indicates how much investors are willing to pay for each dollar of earnings.

P/E Ratio = Market Price per Share / Earnings per Share (EPS)

Alternative calculation:
P/E Ratio = Market Capitalization / Net Income

Types of P/E ratios:

Trailing P/E: Uses earnings from the past 12 months (historical, actual)
Forward P/E: Uses projected earnings for the next 12 months (estimated)

Numerical Example:

Company ABC:
- Current stock price: $75
- Trailing 12-month EPS: $5.00
- Analyst estimated next-year EPS: $6.00

Trailing P/E = $75 / $5.00 = 15x
Forward P/E = $75 / $6.00 = 12.5x

The trailing P/E indicates investors pay $15 for each $1 of current earnings. The lower forward P/E suggests expected earnings growth.

Interpreting P/E ratios:

High P/E (>25):
- Investors expect high future growth
- Stock may be overvalued
- Common in technology and growth sectors

Low P/E (<10):
- Lower growth expectations
- Stock may be undervalued or facing challenges
- Common in mature or cyclical industries

PEG Ratio = P/E / Annual EPS Growth Rate

This adjusts P/E for growth expectations:
If P/E = 20 and growth = 20%: PEG = 1.0 (fairly valued)
If P/E = 20 and growth = 10%: PEG = 2.0 (potentially overvalued)

Limitations:

1. Negative earnings produce meaningless P/E
2. Accounting differences affect EPS comparability
3. Cyclical companies may have misleading P/E at cycle peaks/troughs
4. One-time items distort earnings
5. Different industries have different typical P/E ranges

P/E should be compared to industry peers, historical company averages, and market-wide P/E levels for meaningful analysis.
