Title: Market Capitalization Categories
Date: 2025-03-22
Category: education
Source: Apollo Synthetic Finance Set
Priority: 4

Market capitalization represents the total market value of a company's outstanding shares, serving as a primary classification metric for stocks. It reflects the market's collective valuation of a company's equity.

Market Cap = Current Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding

Standard market cap categories:

Mega-cap: > $200 billion
Large-cap: $10 billion - $200 billion
Mid-cap: $2 billion - $10 billion
Small-cap: $300 million - $2 billion
Micro-cap: $50 million - $300 million
Nano-cap: < $50 million

Numerical Example:

Company XYZ has 500 million shares outstanding, trading at $45 per share.

Market Cap = 500,000,000 × $45 = $22,500,000,000 = $22.5 billion

This classifies XYZ as a large-cap company.

If the share price increases to $60:
New Market Cap = 500,000,000 × $60 = $30 billion

The company remains large-cap but has grown significantly.

Characteristics by category:

Large-cap:
- Established companies with stable revenues
- Lower volatility, lower growth potential
- Higher liquidity, analyst coverage
- Often pay dividends
- Examples: Multinational corporations

Small-cap:
- Younger companies with growth potential
- Higher volatility, higher potential returns
- Lower liquidity, less analyst coverage
- Rarely pay dividends
- Greater sensitivity to economic cycles

Market cap weighting in indexes:

Many indexes use market cap weighting, where larger companies have proportionally greater influence.

S&P 500 example weights:
- $3 trillion company: 6% of index
- $100 billion company: 0.2% of index

Free-float market cap excludes shares not available for public trading (held by insiders, governments), providing a more accurate measure of investable market value.

Market cap changes daily with stock price movements and when companies issue or repurchase shares.
