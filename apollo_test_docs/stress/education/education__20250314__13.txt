Title: Cash Flow Statement Analysis
Date: 2025-03-14
Category: education
Source: Apollo Synthetic Finance Set
Priority: 4

The cash flow statement tracks actual cash movements into and out of a business during a specific period. Unlike the income statement, which uses accrual accounting, the cash flow statement reveals the company's liquidity and cash-generating ability.

Three sections comprise the cash flow statement:

1. Operating Activities: Cash from core business operations
2. Investing Activities: Cash from asset purchases/sales
3. Financing Activities: Cash from debt and equity transactions

Net Change in Cash = Operating CF + Investing CF + Financing CF

The indirect method (most common) starts with net income and adjusts for non-cash items:

Numerical Example:

DEF Corporation Cash Flow Statement (in thousands):

Operating Activities:
Net Income: $150
Add: Depreciation: $50
Add: Decrease in Accounts Receivable: $20
Less: Increase in Inventory: ($30)
Less: Decrease in Accounts Payable: ($10)
Net Cash from Operating: $180

Investing Activities:
Purchase of Equipment: ($200)
Sale of Investments: $50
Net Cash from Investing: ($150)

Financing Activities:
Proceeds from Long-term Debt: $100
Dividends Paid: ($40)
Net Cash from Financing: $60

Net Change in Cash: $180 + ($150) + $60 = $90

Beginning Cash: $50
Ending Cash: $140

Key analysis metrics:

Free Cash Flow (FCF) = Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditures
= $180 - $200 = ($20)

Negative free cash flow indicates the company is investing heavily, which may be positive for growth but requires external funding.

Operating Cash Flow Ratio = Operating CF / Current Liabilities

This measures ability to cover short-term obligations with cash generated from operations.

Cash flow analysis reveals:
- Quality of earnings (high net income but low operating cash flow is a warning sign)
- Capital investment patterns
- Financing strategy and dividend sustainability
- Liquidity trends over time
