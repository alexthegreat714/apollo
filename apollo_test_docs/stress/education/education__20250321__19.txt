Title: Tax-Loss Harvesting Strategies
Date: 2025-03-21
Category: education
Source: Apollo Synthetic Finance Set
Priority: 4

Tax-loss harvesting is a strategy that realizes investment losses to offset capital gains, thereby reducing tax liability. The practice involves selling securities at a loss and reinvesting proceeds in similar (but not substantially identical) securities to maintain market exposure.

The core tax benefit calculation:

Tax Savings = Capital Loss × Marginal Tax Rate

Capital gains tax rates (for long-term gains held over one year) are typically lower than ordinary income rates. Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income.

Numerical Example:

Portfolio situation:
- Stock A: Purchased at $10,000, current value $7,000 (unrealized loss: $3,000)
- Stock B: Purchased at $5,000, current value $8,000 (unrealized gain: $3,000)
- Marginal tax rate: 24% (ordinary), 15% (long-term capital gains)

Strategy: Sell Stock A to realize the $3,000 loss.

If Stock B is sold (long-term gain):
Tax on gain without harvesting: $3,000 × 15% = $450
Tax on gain with harvesting: ($3,000 - $3,000) × 15% = $0
Tax savings: $450

If no gains to offset, losses can:
1. Offset up to $3,000 of ordinary income annually
2. Carry forward indefinitely to future years

Ordinary income offset value: $3,000 × 24% = $720

Wash Sale Rule:

The IRS disallows the loss if substantially identical securities are purchased within 30 days before or after the sale. This creates a 61-day window (30 days before, sale day, 30 days after).

Compliant alternatives:
- Different company in same sector
- ETF instead of individual stock
- Similar but not identical index fund

Considerations:

1. Transaction costs may reduce benefits
2. Future tax rates affect value of deferred gains
3. State taxes may add additional savings
4. Carryforward losses reduce future flexibility
5. Resets cost basis, affecting future gains

Tax-loss harvesting is most valuable in taxable accounts. Retirement accounts are tax-deferred, making the strategy inapplicable.
