OPTIONS FUNDAMENTALS

An option is a contract giving the buyer the right, but not obligation, to buy (call) or sell (put) an asset at a specified price (strike) by a certain date (expiration). Options provide leverage, hedging capability, and income generation but require understanding of multiple risk factors.

BASIC STRATEGIES

Long Call: Bullish bet with limited loss (premium paid) and unlimited upside
Long Put: Bearish bet with limited loss (premium paid) and substantial downside protection
Covered Call: Own stock and sell call to generate income, capping upside
Protective Put: Own stock and buy put for downside protection (insurance)

Options Strategy Profiles:
-----------------------------------
Strategy       | Max Profit | Max Loss | Break Even
Long Call      | Unlimited  | Premium  | Strike + Premium
Long Put       | Strike-Prem| Premium  | Strike - Premium
Covered Call   | Strike+Prem| Stock-Prem| Stock - Premium
Protective Put | Unlimited  | Stock-Str+Prem | Stock + Premium
-----------------------------------

OPTION PRICING

Option prices reflect intrinsic value (in-the-money amount) and time value (premium above intrinsic). Time value decays as expiration approaches (theta). Prices increase with volatility (vega) and time to expiration. Interest rates and dividends also affect prices but are usually secondary factors.

THE GREEKS

Delta measures price sensitivity to the underlying asset
Gamma measures how delta changes as the underlying moves
Theta measures time decay per day
Vega measures sensitivity to implied volatility changes

SPREAD STRATEGIES

Spreads involve buying and selling options simultaneously to reduce cost and limit risk.
Bull Call Spread: Buy lower strike call, sell higher strike call (bullish, limited risk)
Bear Put Spread: Buy higher strike put, sell lower strike put (bearish, limited risk)
Iron Condor: Sell put spread and call spread for income in range-bound markets

RISK CONSIDERATIONS

Options can expire worthless, resulting in 100% loss of premium. Selling options creates unlimited risk without proper hedging. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses. Liquidity varies significantly across strikes and expirations.
