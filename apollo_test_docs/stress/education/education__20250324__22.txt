Title: Beta and Systematic Risk
Date: 2025-03-24
Category: education
Source: Apollo Synthetic Finance Set
Priority: 4

Beta measures a security's sensitivity to market movements, quantifying systematic risk that cannot be eliminated through diversification. It is a key component in the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) for estimating required returns.

Beta formula:

β = Covariance(Ri, Rm) / Variance(Rm)

Or equivalently:
β = ρ(i,m) × (σi / σm)

Where:
- Ri = Return of the security
- Rm = Return of the market
- ρ = Correlation between security and market
- σ = Standard deviation

Numerical Example:

Stock XYZ historical analysis:
- Standard deviation of XYZ: 30%
- Standard deviation of market: 18%
- Correlation between XYZ and market: 0.72

β = 0.72 × (0.30 / 0.18)
β = 0.72 × 1.667
β = 1.20

Interpreting beta values:

β = 1.0: Security moves with the market
β > 1.0: Security is more volatile than market (aggressive)
β < 1.0: Security is less volatile than market (defensive)
β = 0: No correlation with market
β < 0: Inverse correlation (rare)

For XYZ with β = 1.20:
If market rises 10%, XYZ expected to rise 12%
If market falls 10%, XYZ expected to fall 12%

CAPM application:

Required Return = Rf + β(Rm - Rf)

Where (Rm - Rf) is the market risk premium.

If Rf = 3%, expected market return = 9%:
Required Return = 3% + 1.20(9% - 3%)
= 3% + 7.2%
= 10.2%

Portfolio beta:

βp = Σ(wi × βi)

A portfolio with 50% in Stock A (β=1.5) and 50% in Stock B (β=0.8):
βp = 0.50(1.5) + 0.50(0.8) = 1.15

Limitations:

1. Beta is backward-looking and may not predict future behavior
2. Assumes linear relationship with market
3. Market proxy selection affects calculation
4. Beta changes over time
5. Does not capture unsystematic risk

High-beta stocks are typically cyclical companies, while low-beta stocks include utilities and consumer staples.
