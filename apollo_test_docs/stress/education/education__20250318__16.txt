Title: Bond Duration and Interest Rate Sensitivity
Date: 2025-03-18
Category: education
Source: Apollo Synthetic Finance Set
Priority: 4

Duration measures a bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes, expressed in years. It represents the weighted average time until a bondholder receives the bond's cash flows, with weights proportional to present values.

Macaulay Duration formula:

D = Σ[t × PV(CFt)] / Bond Price

Where t is the time period and PV(CFt) is the present value of the cash flow at time t.

Modified Duration converts Macaulay Duration into a direct measure of price sensitivity:

Modified Duration = Macaulay Duration / (1 + y/n)

Where y is yield to maturity and n is compounding frequency per year.

Price change approximation:

ΔP/P ≈ -Modified Duration × Δy

Numerical Example:

Consider a 3-year bond with 5% annual coupon, $1,000 face value, and 4% yield to maturity:

Year 1 cash flow: $50, PV = $50/1.04 = $48.08
Year 2 cash flow: $50, PV = $50/1.04² = $46.23
Year 3 cash flow: $1,050, PV = $1,050/1.04³ = $933.51

Bond Price = $48.08 + $46.23 + $933.51 = $1,027.82

Macaulay Duration = [(1 × $48.08) + (2 × $46.23) + (3 × $933.51)] / $1,027.82
= [$48.08 + $92.46 + $2,800.53] / $1,027.82
= $2,941.07 / $1,027.82
= 2.86 years

Modified Duration = 2.86 / 1.04 = 2.75

If interest rates increase by 1% (100 basis points):
ΔP/P ≈ -2.75 × 0.01 = -2.75%

The bond price would decrease approximately 2.75%.

Factors affecting duration:

1. Maturity: Longer maturity increases duration
2. Coupon rate: Higher coupons decrease duration
3. Yield: Higher yields decrease duration

Zero-coupon bonds have duration equal to maturity. Duration is essential for immunization strategies and managing interest rate risk in fixed income portfolios.
