Title: US Homebuilder Industry Analysis
Date: 2025-03-05
Category: micro
Source: Raymond James Building Products Research

INDUSTRY FUNDAMENTALS

The US homebuilding industry completed 1.0 million single-family homes in 2024, below normalized demand of 1.3-1.4 million driven by household formation and replacement. Builders navigate constrained lot supply, labor shortages, and interest rate sensitivity while benefiting from severe existing home inventory shortage.

COMPETITIVE POSITIONING

The top 10 builders account for 45% of new home sales, up from 30% a decade ago. Scale advantages in land acquisition, purchasing, and consumer financing drive consolidation. D.R. Horton leads with 90,000+ annual closings followed by Lennar and PulteGroup.

DEMAND DRIVERS

Underlying demand remains strong from demographics (millennials entering prime homebuying age), housing deficit (3-4 million units), and obsolescence. Affordability constrains effective demand at current rates. Builders address affordability through smaller floor plans and spec home efficiency.

COST STRUCTURE

Direct construction costs have moderated from 2021-2022 peaks but remain elevated. Labor shortages persist despite wage increases. Lumber and other materials have normalized. Land costs continue rising as developed lots become scarcer.

FINANCING AND INCENTIVES

Builders subsidize mortgage rates through forward commitments, typically offering 5.5-6.0% rates versus market rates of 6.5%+. These incentives cost 3-5% of home price but drive sales velocity. Attached financing advantages create competitive moat versus existing home sellers.

MARGIN OUTLOOK

Gross margins have compressed from peak 28% to 24% as incentives increased. We expect margins to stabilize as rate environment improves and incentive costs decline. Operating leverage maintains strong returns on capital despite margin pressure.
