Title: China Economic Outlook - Structural Headwinds and Policy Response
Date: 2025-03-10
Category: macro
Source: World Bank China Economic Update

GROWTH OUTLOOK

China's GDP growth is projected at 4.5% for 2025 and 4.2% for 2026, below the government's 5% target but representing stabilization after 2024's slowdown. The economy faces structural headwinds from property sector adjustment, demographic aging, and geopolitical tensions that constrain the recovery's magnitude.

PROPERTY SECTOR ADJUSTMENT

Real estate investment declined 10% in 2024 and will contract another 5-8% in 2025 despite policy support. Housing starts are down 60% from peak and unlikely to recover to previous levels. The property sector's contribution to GDP has declined from 29% to under 20%. This adjustment is necessary but creates persistent drag on growth.

CONSUMER RECOVERY

Household consumption is recovering gradually from pandemic restrictions and property wealth effects. Retail sales growth is projected to accelerate to 5% as confidence improves. However, the household savings rate remains elevated at 33% versus pre-pandemic 30%, suggesting ongoing caution.

POLICY RESPONSE

Fiscal stimulus of 3 trillion yuan targets infrastructure, consumer subsidies, and local government debt resolution. Monetary policy remains accommodative with rates at historic lows. Property policies have relaxed purchase restrictions and lowered mortgage rates.

EXTERNAL PRESSURES

Export growth faces headwinds from trade restrictions, particularly in semiconductors and EVs. Manufacturing overcapacity creates trade tensions with developed markets. Nearshoring trends benefit competitors in Southeast Asia, Mexico, and India.

MEDIUM-TERM OUTLOOK

Structural reforms are necessary to sustain growth above 4%. These include SOE reform, services sector opening, social safety net expansion, and innovation promotion. Absent reform acceleration, potential growth will decline toward 3% by 2030.
