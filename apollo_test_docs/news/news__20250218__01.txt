Title: Federal Reserve Signals Patience on Rate Cuts Amid Sticky Inflation
Date: 2025-02-18
Category: news
Source: Financial Times

Federal Reserve officials indicated they are in no rush to cut interest rates as recent inflation data showed prices rising faster than expected. January's Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% month-over-month, pushing the annual rate to 3.1%, above the anticipated 2.9%.

Fed Governor Christopher Waller stated that "the data suggests we should hold rates steady and wait for more evidence that inflation is moving sustainably toward our 2% target." Markets have pushed back expectations for the first rate cut from June to September 2025.

The 10-year Treasury yield jumped 12 basis points to 4.35% following the inflation report, while the S&P 500 fell 1.4% on concerns that higher-for-longer rates would pressure corporate earnings. The dollar strengthened against major currencies as interest rate differentials widened.

Economists noted that shelter costs remained the primary driver of inflation, accounting for over half of the monthly increase. However, goods prices also rose after several months of declines, raising concerns about renewed pricing pressures in the supply chain.

Market participants will closely watch the February inflation report and upcoming Fed communications for signals on the timing and pace of rate reductions this year.
