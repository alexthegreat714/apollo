Title: Oil Prices Surge on Middle East Tensions and Supply Concerns
Date: 2025-02-08
Category: news
Source: Reuters

Brent crude oil jumped 5% to $86 per barrel as escalating tensions in the Middle East raised concerns about potential supply disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz. West Texas Intermediate rose to $81, the highest level since October 2024.

Shipping companies have rerouted vessels around the Cape of Good Hope to avoid Red Sea attacks, adding two weeks to Asia-Europe transit times and increasing transportation costs. Container rates have tripled from pre-crisis levels.

OPEC+ maintained its production cuts of 2.2 million barrels per day, with Saudi Arabia indicating it would not increase output until inventories decline further. Russian exports have remained stable despite Western sanctions as Asian buyers absorb displaced barrels.

US gasoline prices rose to $3.45 per gallon nationally, up 15% from December lows. Analysts project prices could reach $4.00 by summer driving season if geopolitical risks persist and demand remains strong.

Energy stocks outperformed the broader market, with the S&P 500 Energy sector gaining 4.2% for the week. Exxon and Chevron led gainers as investors positioned for sustained higher oil prices.
