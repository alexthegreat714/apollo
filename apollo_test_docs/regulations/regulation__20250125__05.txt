Title: Anti-Money Laundering Requirements for Investment Advisers
Date: 2025-01-25
Category: regulation
Source: Financial Crimes Enforcement Network

PROPOSED RULE OVERVIEW

FinCEN has proposed to add certain investment advisers to the definition of "financial institution" under the Bank Secrecy Act. Covered investment advisers would be subject to comprehensive AML/CFT program requirements and suspicious activity reporting obligations.

COVERED ADVISERS

The rule would apply to SEC-registered investment advisers and advisers that report to the SEC as exempt reporting advisers. State-registered advisers without SEC registration would not be covered in the initial phase.

AML PROGRAM REQUIREMENTS

Covered advisers must implement risk-based AML programs including: written policies and procedures, designation of a compliance officer, ongoing employee training, and independent testing. Programs must be tailored to the adviser's business model and client types.

CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION PROGRAM

Advisers must implement Customer Identification Programs (CIP) requiring collection and verification of client identity information. For legal entity clients, this includes identifying beneficial owners with 25% or greater ownership and individuals with control of the entity.

SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORTING

Advisers must file Suspicious Activity Reports for transactions of $5,000 or more that the adviser knows or suspects involve funds from illegal activity, are designed to evade reporting requirements, or lack a lawful purpose. Reports must be filed within 30 calendar days of detection.

IMPLEMENTATION PERIOD

The final rule is expected to provide a 12-month compliance period from the effective date. Advisers should begin assessing their risk profiles, client bases, and technology needs in anticipation of the requirements.
