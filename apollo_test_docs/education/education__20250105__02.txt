Title: Tax-Advantaged Retirement Accounts Explained
Date: 2025-01-05
Category: education
Source: Internal Revenue Service Publication 590

TRADITIONAL IRA

Contributions to a traditional IRA may be tax-deductible depending on your income and whether you have a retirement plan at work. Earnings grow tax-deferred, and you pay ordinary income tax on withdrawals in retirement. Required minimum distributions begin at age 73.

ROTH IRA

Roth IRA contributions are made with after-tax dollars - there is no tax deduction. However, qualified withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free, including all earnings. There are no required minimum distributions during your lifetime.

401(K) PLANS

A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement plan that allows you to contribute pre-tax dollars from your paycheck. Many employers match a portion of your contributions - this is essentially free money. The 2025 contribution limit is $23,500, with an additional $7,500 catch-up contribution for those 50 and older.

ROTH 401(K)

Similar to a Roth IRA, a Roth 401(k) allows after-tax contributions with tax-free qualified withdrawals. The contribution limits are the same as traditional 401(k)s. Employer matching contributions go into a traditional (pre-tax) account.

CONTRIBUTION LIMITS 2025

Traditional/Roth IRA: $7,000 ($8,000 if 50+)
401(k)/403(b): $23,500 ($31,000 if 50+)
SEP IRA: Up to 25% of compensation, maximum $69,000

Maximize tax-advantaged accounts before investing in taxable accounts to optimize long-term wealth building.
