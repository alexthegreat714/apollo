Title: Estate Planning Fundamentals
Date: 2025-01-20
Category: education
Source: American Bar Association Consumer Guide

WHY ESTATE PLANNING MATTERS

Estate planning ensures your assets are distributed according to your wishes after death and can minimize taxes and legal complications for your heirs. Everyone with assets or dependents needs an estate plan, regardless of wealth.

ESSENTIAL DOCUMENTS

Will: Specifies how assets should be distributed and names guardians for minor children.
Durable Power of Attorney: Authorizes someone to manage your finances if you are incapacitated.
Healthcare Proxy: Designates someone to make medical decisions if you cannot.
Living Will: Specifies your wishes for end-of-life medical care.

TRUSTS

A trust holds assets for beneficiaries according to your instructions. Revocable living trusts avoid probate and maintain privacy, while irrevocable trusts can reduce estate taxes. Trusts can include conditions on distributions, such as age requirements or educational achievements.

BENEFICIARY DESIGNATIONS

Retirement accounts and life insurance pass by beneficiary designation, not your will. Review and update these regularly, especially after life changes like marriage, divorce, or having children. Outdated designations can override your will.

ESTATE AND GIFT TAXES

The 2025 federal estate tax exemption is $13.99 million per person. Estates below this threshold owe no federal estate tax. Annual gift exclusion allows tax-free gifts up to $19,000 per recipient without reducing your lifetime exemption.

Review your estate plan every 3-5 years or after major life events. State laws vary significantly - consult an attorney in your state.
