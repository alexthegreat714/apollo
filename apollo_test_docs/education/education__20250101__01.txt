Title: Understanding Asset Allocation and Portfolio Diversification
Date: 2025-01-01
Category: education
Source: Investor Education Foundation

WHAT IS ASSET ALLOCATION?

Asset allocation is the process of dividing your investments among different asset categories, such as stocks, bonds, and cash. The mix you choose will depend on your investment goals, time horizon, and tolerance for risk.

THE IMPORTANCE OF DIVERSIFICATION

Diversification reduces risk by spreading investments across various assets that are unlikely to all move in the same direction at the same time. A diversified portfolio may not produce the highest returns, but it typically reduces volatility and protects against severe losses.

MAJOR ASSET CLASSES

Stocks (equities) represent ownership in companies and offer growth potential but with higher volatility. Bonds (fixed income) are loans to governments or corporations that provide regular income with lower risk. Cash and equivalents offer stability and liquidity but limited growth potential.

DETERMINING YOUR ALLOCATION

Consider your investment timeline: longer horizons allow for more stock exposure since there is time to recover from downturns. Assess your risk tolerance honestly - the best allocation is one you can stick with during market turbulence. Review and rebalance annually.

SAMPLE ALLOCATIONS

Conservative (income-focused): 30% stocks, 50% bonds, 20% cash
Moderate (balanced): 60% stocks, 30% bonds, 10% cash
Aggressive (growth-focused): 80% stocks, 15% bonds, 5% cash

These are starting points - adjust based on your individual circumstances and consult a financial advisor for personalized guidance.
